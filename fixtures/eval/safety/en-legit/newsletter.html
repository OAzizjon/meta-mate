Preparing Your Perennials for the First Hard Frost

Dear Fellow Gardeners, as nighttime temperatures start dipping toward freezing, late autumn is the time to give your perennial beds a restful transition into the dormant months. A little patience now means strong, vibrant blooms next May.

Tip of the Week: Don't Cut Everything Down Leave ornamental grasses and coneflower seed heads standing through the winter months. They provide vital shelter and natural seeds for neighborhood birds like goldfinches and chickadees when snow covers the ground.

Mulching Strategy for Cold Winters

Wait until the ground has had its first solid freeze before applying a 3-to-4 inch layer of straw, shredded leaves, or wood chips. Putting mulch down too early can keep the ground artificially warm and attract nesting rodents looking for shelter.
